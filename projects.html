01 · Klook
2020 — Present Distribution · Partnerships · APAC

Distribution at the speed of attention.

Strategic partnerships at the largest experience marketplace in Asia. Attractions, tours, transit and rail — the commerce of how a place is actually visited.

Klook is the part of travel most people never see — the booking on the train platform, the QR for the temple, the rail pass that just works. The job is unglamorous, deeply operational, and entirely about partnerships: with operators, with attractions, with cities themselves.

The role spans market entry, supply, and the slow build of trust with thousands of operators across the region. The lesson, after a few years of it: nothing scales in this industry without a phone call.

"Experience commerce is the most honest part of travel marketing. Either the guest scans the QR — or they don't."
— On the work at Klook

02 · PATA
2026 — 2028 Industry · Governance · Asia-Pacific

A seat at the regional table.

Elected to the Board of the Pacific Asia Travel Association — the region's most enduring industry body, in its 75th year.

PATA convenes the governments, airlines, hotels and operators that shape how the region travels. The board sets the direction; the convenings — the Annual Summit, the workshops, the destination roundtables — set the tone.

Keynote at the 75th PATA Annual Summit, South Korea, May 2026. Standing committee work on partnerships and emerging markets. A two-year term to listen carefully, and contribute usefully.

"Industry bodies are at their best when they're a room you'd want to be in anyway."
— On joining the PATA Board

03 · HBX Group
2017 — 2020 B2B Distribution · Bedbank · MarketHub Asia

The plumbing
of hospitality.

Bedbank and B2B distribution across the region. Contracts that perform, partnerships that hold, and the annual convening of the Asian trade at MarketHub Asia.

HBX Group (formerly Hotelbeds) is the largest B2B travel marketplace in the world — the wholesaler behind hundreds of OTAs, tour operators and corporate platforms. The work was contract architecture: 5,000-room portfolios, dynamic pricing, channel discipline, and a lot of careful reading.

MarketHub Asia is the company's flagship trade convening, drawing 600+ delegates a year across hospitality, distribution, and destination marketing. Co-shaping that room — its programming, its speakers, its quieter dinners — was as much of the job as the contracts themselves.

"The best contract is the one neither side needs to read again."
— A house principle from the HBX years

04 · TAT · Singapore
2026 — In progress Destination marketing · Government · DMO

Thailand,
read from Singapore.

Strategic marketing & partnership consultancy to the Tourism Authority of Thailand, Singapore Office — the destination's gateway to a 2.8M-traveller market.

A three-month assessment, then a year. The brief: audit the Singapore-Thailand corridor, sharpen positioning against Vietnam, Malaysia and Indonesia, and rebuild the partnership pipeline with airlines, OTAs, and luxury trade.

The work is quietly operational: weekly meetings, monthly readouts, a calendar built around shoulder seasons, and a renewed contract with the travel trade ecosystem that actually moves visitors.

"Destinations don't need more campaigns. They need more honest readouts of what's already working."
— Opening note to TAT Singapore

05 · Luxury · LV → SHA
2012 — 2017 Luxury hospitality · Operations · Sales

Las Vegas to Shanghai,
a luxury apprenticeship.

Five years inside luxury hospitality at scale — from the Las Vegas Strip to a residence on the Bund, learning the trade from the inside of the lobby out.

An education in operations: front office, sales, corporate accounts, the long apprenticeship of how a five-star property actually runs an evening. Begun on a Singapore Hotel Association scholarship at UNLV — Hotel Administration, 2009–2012 — and continued at properties across two of the most demanding luxury markets in the world.

The lesson that's never left the work: marketing in hospitality is downstream of the lobby. If the door is held well, the rest is easier.

"You can't write a brand story for a hotel you haven't slept in."
— A working principle since
